## Non-negotiable invariants (read this before anything else)

Alex compiled this list specifically so it's explicit and complete before any more design work happens — treat every item below as a hard requirement the plan must guarantee, not an aspiration. These are distinct in *kind* from the knob taxonomy that follows: the taxonomy is about the physical story's plausibility (a strong preference, and the thing that bends first when it conflicts with anything here); this list is about matching the already-decided result, which — per the mission constraint below — must never be contradicted, full stop.

1. A fielder credited with catching a ball in the air must reach it before it hits the ground.
2. Every ground-archetype out (`GROUND_ARCHETYPES` — GO/DP/FC/TP family, not just a literal groundout) must be fielded within the infield dirt.
3. On a play where a baserunner is safe at a base, that runner must beat both the ball and the fielder there. True **independently for every runner on a multi-out play**, not just the most prominent one.
4. On a play where a baserunner is out at a base, the ball and the fielder must beat that runner there — same independent-per-runner requirement as #3. Two genuinely different physical requirements live under this one line, not one mechanism twice:
   - **Force out**: a fielder must actually be at (or covering) the base *and have caught/received the ball* before the runner arrives — not just "the ball" reaching the base's coordinates in the abstract. Someone has to be there to receive it.
   - **Tag out**: the fielder must additionally physically apply the tag before the runner reaches safely.
5. A fielder can't throw a ball until they have the ball.
6. A fielder can't run with a ball until they have the ball.
   - Both of these generalize to one rule: a fielder can't act on something they haven't received yet. Already-coded instances of the same rule: a catcher can't release a steal throw before actually receiving the pitch; a cutoff/relay receiver can't re-throw before their own catch happens.
7. Only one baseball exists on the field at a time.
8. A trailing runner can never pass a lead runner still ahead of them on the bases.
9. A runner tagging up can't be shown leaving the bag before the ball is actually caught.
10. A ball that clears the fence in the air is always a home run — but **not every home run clears the fence in the air**. An inside-the-park home run is a real, legitimate outcome (the ball stays in play; the batter and any runners simply circle the bases without being retired) and must never be forced to look like it left the park, and never treated as an error. The one-directional rule that actually holds: non-home-run results may never clear the fence; home run results are never forced to.
11. A relay throw can't leave before the previous throw in the chain has landed.
12. Exactly the runners the recorded result says score — no more, no fewer — cross home.
13. No token, ball or fielder, may ever visibly teleport. Every position change is continuous, real-paced motion (this is the throughline behind three separate fixes from this session: dropping the old "camped" reprojection, the ball-trail clamp, and the render/physics consistency fix — worth reading as one general rule rather than three unrelated patches).

Cross-reference against the tasks below as you design: #3/#4's independent-per-runner requirement is exactly what Task 2 is for; #4's force-out sub-case (a fielder physically there to receive it) is exactly what Task 3 is for. Neither task is "done" unless its own design can be checked directly against the specific invariant here.

## Alex's own knob taxonomy (organizing framework for this whole review)

Alex's own list, six categories, verbatim in spirit: **fielder positioning (depth, angle)**, **fielder movement speed**, **fielder throw speed**, **fielder delay** (movement-start delay, and the separate delay before throwing after fielding/catching as part of a throw sequence), **runner delay**, **runner speed**.

Alex's own instinct, treat this as a real design requirement for the whole review, not a suggestion: **most or all of these should be tunable in both directions** — bounded speed-ups where honest physics falls short of a guaranteed out, and equally real, bounded slow-downs where honest physics would otherwise turn a recorded hit into what looks like an easy out. Some of these already have real, data-grounded bounds (`THROW_SPEED_BY_POS`'s per-position min/mph/max, `spdPaceScale`'s per-player SPD scaling); others don't yet and may need their own calibration pass before they're trustworthy in both directions rather than tuned by feel per play.

Mapping the six categories against what's actually built today, before you do your own audit:

- **Positioning (depth, angle)** — depth is bidirectional-in-spirit for outs only (section A #3: deeper only, by explicit design — a fielder is never shown shallower than their real default). Angle-only bidirectionality exists for outfield hits (`ofDerivedShadeAnchorFt` shifts bearing away from the ball to honestly lose a pursuit). There's no depth-side equivalent for hits (an outfielder shaded too shallow or too deep to plausibly reach a ball) — decide whether that's a real gap or unnecessary given angle-shading already covers the outfield-hit case.
- **Movement speed** — **not a direct, standalone knob today.** "Faster" is only ever achieved *indirectly*, by shrinking the distance a fielder has to cover (the depth/bearing leeway in section A) — `FIELDER_CHARGE_FT_PER_S`/`OF_CHARGE_FT_PER_S` themselves are never scaled per-play. "Slower" doesn't exist at all outside the outfield-pursuit read-delay. This is the most actionable gap in the taxonomy — see Task 4.
- **Throw speed** — the one category that's genuinely bidirectional and bounded today: `slowThrow`/`speedThrow`, both solving within `THROW_SPEED_BY_POS[pos].min/max`.
- **Delay, movement-start** — `CHARGE_REACTION_S`/`PITCHER_CHARGE_REACTION_S` exist but are flat constants, never adjusted per-play. `OF_READ_DELAY_MAX_MS` is the one real per-play, bounded "delay this fielder's own read" knob, and it's outfield-only.
- **Delay, post-catch/field pre-throw** — `THROW_DELAY_MS`/`CATCHER_POP_MS`/`CUTOFF_TRANSFER_MS` as the baseline, `quickRelease` shrinking it, `holdRelease` extending it — genuinely bidirectional already, though `holdRelease`'s own bound (`HOLD_RELEASE_MAX_MS`, effectively unbounded at 20000ms) is worth a second look given how much of the rest of this document is about giving every other knob a real ceiling.
- **Runner delay** — `runnerLateJump` (later break, bounded) exists; `hustleRunner` behaves like an earlier-effective-arrival knob but isn't framed as a pure delay adjustment the same way — decide whether that's a real second knob or the same thing described two ways.
- **Runner speed** — real per-player SPD already scales `RUNNER_SPRINT_FT_PER_S` (`runnerSpd`/`RUNNER_SPD_FT_PER_S_PER_POINT`); `stretchRunner`/`hustleRunner` are the reconciliation-side bounded adjustments on top of that, both already bidirectional.

**Two more axes worth your own consideration, not on Alex's original list — Claude's addition, weigh them rather than assuming they belong:**
- **Acceleration, separate from top speed.** `FIELDER_ACCEL_FT_S2`/`RUNNER_ACCEL_FT_S2`/`OF_PURSUIT_ACCEL_FT_S2` are flat constants today — never per-player, never per-play. A fielder with a quick first step but modest top speed is a genuinely different profile from a fast-but-slow-to-ramp-up one, and on a bang-bang play the ramp-up rate can matter as much as top speed does. Decide whether this belongs in scope now or is a deliberate later stretch.
- **An infield-specific reaction/read-delay for hits**, mirroring `OF_READ_DELAY_MAX_MS` but for infielders — today the delay category's hit-direction knob is outfield-only; an infield hit has no equivalent "read it late" lever at all, and relies entirely on the honest race's own conservative pace to miss convincingly.

## Current state — what shipped this session (the actual audit subject)

### A. The fielding/anchor layer

Ground-ball fielding used to have a "camped" mechanism: whichever fielder the HZ-angle table nominally assigned a grounder to raced from a *reprojected* point (on the ball's own line, at that position's canonical depth), not their real static anchor — a teleport, not a race. That's gone. Every infield/outfield charge candidate now races from their own real `FIELDER_ANCHORS_FT[pos]`, unconditionally, via `chargeInIntercept`. Layered on top of that honest race, in order of when each was added:

1. **Infield-dirt guarantee.** `resolveGrounderInterception`'s `maxAlongFt` — previously only applied to `bunt`/`infield_single` (`STAYS_IN_INFIELD_ARCHETYPES`) — now applies to plain `"grounder"` too, via the broader `GROUND_ARCHETYPES` table. Every ground-archetype race (which is always a real, recorded out — `GROUND_ARCHETYPES` maps DP/FC/GO/TP-family results, confirmed against `result_diff_bands.csv`) is bounded so the winner is credited no deeper than `dirtEdgeFt(flight.angle)`. Not circular: `dirtEdgeFt` depends only on the ball's own angle/distance, never on who's racing there, so it can be computed before the race runs.
2. **`WINNER_CHEAT_MAX_DEG` (currently 3°).** When (and only when) the honest race never achieves a genuine mid-roll crossing — it hits `fielderInterceptS`'s own `{alongFt: restFt, atS: chargeFielderArriveS(...)}` fallback — the winner's rendered start bearing gets nudged up to this many degrees toward the ball, via `capBearingTowardFt`. Deliberately small (well under the 8° width of one HZ bucket) so it never reads as teleporting into a neighboring zone. Not yet eyeballed against real plays — flagged as a starting guess in its own comment.
3. **Depth leeway**, same fallback-only condition as #2. The winner's start depth gets pushed out — never past their own real `dirtEdgeFt` at the bearing #2 landed on, and never past the target point's own depth either — from their real `INFIELDER_DEPTH_FT[pos]`. Explicit `Math.max`/`Math.min` ordering guarantees it only ever moves deeper, never shallower, than the position's real default depth (Alex's own explicit requirement, confirmed by test/probe this session, not just asserted).
4. **Honest re-timing.** Once #2/#3 relocate the start point, `groundTimeS` is recomputed from *that* point via `gp.timeAt(fieldedFt)` — not just relabeled. Guarded against the `trajectory.js` null-on-overshoot behavior noted above.
5. **Whole-infield depth shift (`flight.infieldDepthPct`).** Once the winner's depth leeway (#3) is known, the fraction of *their own* room used — `(adjustedDepth − theirDefault) / (theirOwnDirtEdge − theirDefault)` — is stored on `flight` and applied to every *other* infielder's own idle start position in `fielderStartAnchorFt`, each against *their own* default depth and *their own* dirt edge, bearing untouched. Explicitly excludes the pitcher (table entry exists, `INFIELDER_DEPTH_FT.P`, but real pitchers don't set up deeper pre-pitch) and the catcher (excluded for free — no depth concept in the data at all).
6. **Ball-trail clamp** (`groundPhaseSamples`). Before this session, a sample's ground position always came from `gp.distAt(t)` — the ball's own *unconstrained* natural roll — except the very last sample, which was snapped to `fieldedFt`. Once #1–#4 above could credit a fielded point *earlier* than the ball's own natural roll would reach it (the fallback case), that mismatch became visible: the ball's trail would roll past the fielded point, then snap back for the final frame. Now every sample clamps to `fieldedFt` once the natural roll reaches it — the ball visually stops and waits, same convention already used for a ball that legitimately comes to rest before its fielder ambles over.
7. **Render/physics consistency** (`chainMoverPlan`). The ball-toucher's own rendered leg used `profileKind: "run"` (`RUNNER_SPRINT_FT_PER_S`, no reaction beat) regardless of archetype — a materially faster, differently-shaped motion profile than the `"charge"` kind (`FIELDER_CHARGE_FT_PER_S`, real reaction beat) the race itself used to decide who fields it and when. Now `GROUND_ARCHETYPES` plays render the ball-toucher with `"charge"`, matching the race's own physics exactly.

**Audit questions for this layer:** Is #2/#3/#5 (three separate mechanisms, all gated on the same "no genuine crossing" condition, all touching `fielderStartAnchorFt`) the simplest expression of "the defense visibly set up deep for this batter," or should they be unified into one function/one data flow? Is `flight.infieldDepthPct` living as an ad hoc property on the `flight` object the right shape, or does it belong somewhere more structured now that the fielding layer has this many interdependent per-play adjustments? Does the sequencing — bearing (#2), then depth off the *bearing-adjusted* point (#3), then re-time (#4), then propagate a scalar derived from #3 to every other fielder (#5) — have any order-dependence risk if a future change touches one step without re-deriving the next?

### B. The reconciliation layer

This is the pooled water-fill system from the last plan (`reconcileThrowSchedule`, `allocateAcrossKnobs`, `MARGIN_POLICY`), largely unchanged in shape, but stressed this session in two ways:

1. **Delay constants raised toward real values**, following an MLB catcher pop-time comparison (~0.73s real average): `THROW_DELAY_MS` 60→500, `CATCHER_POP_MS` 250→750, `CUTOFF_TRANSFER_MS` 150→500, `THROW_STAGGER_MS` 50→500. These are all real increases to the *un-reconciled* baseline every schedule starts from — meaning the reconciler now has to close much bigger gaps on some real plays than it was tuned against.
2. **New `speedThrow` knob**, mirroring the existing `slowThrow` (which eases a throw's mph down toward `THROW_SPEED_BY_POS[pos].min` when a throw needs to land *later*). `speedThrow` solves toward `THROW_SPEED_BY_POS[pos].max` when a throw needs to land *earlier* — the direction that was previously covered only by `quickRelease` (shrinks transfer/stagger gaps) pooled equally with `runnerLateJump`/`stretchRunner`, even though the latter two only change how the *runner's own rendered token* looks and never touch the schedule at all. `speedThrow` sits outside that pool (added after `quickRelease`, using whatever `quickRelease` didn't cover), exactly mirroring how `slowThrow` already sits outside its own pool relative to `hustleRunner`. It's also written to loop the *whole* throw chain, final leg first cascading backward through earlier legs, not just the final leg — a multi-leg relay (a double or triple play) can speed up more than one throw if more than one needs it, each capped at *that* leg's own thrower's real ceiling.
3. **The "unresolved residual" check was reworked** to measure `last.endMs − required` directly off the schedule's own final state, rather than reconstructing it by subtracting each pooled knob's *credited* share (which double-counted knobs that don't actually move the schedule).

**The one finding from this session that most needs your attention:** `throwSchedule`'s own comment states outright that *"a relay's earlier legs are real chained transfers, only the last leg ever actually races anyone."* Concretely: on a double play with two force-outs (say, a throw home forcing the runner from third, then a relay to second forcing the batter), `reconcileThrowSchedule` is only ever called once, against the *final* leg and *one* runner. The first leg — which is also, per the recorded result, retiring a specific runner at a specific base — has no reconciliation guarantee at all today. It renders at whatever the honest chained-transfer timing produces, with no check that it actually beats the runner it's supposedly putting out. This was true before this session (it's the original design, not something introduced this session) but the delay-constant increases in point 1 make it materially more likely to matter in practice, and Alex has separately stated the "fielder/throw beats the runner" invariant is non-negotiable for every recorded out, not just the last one in a chain.

**Also still open, not yet started:** covering fielders (1B on a grounder hit elsewhere, or any fielder covering a base for a force/tag not tied to the ball itself) need a guaranteed "arrives and can receive the throw before the runner" property, symmetric to the "glove beats the ball" guarantee section A already has for fielding a grounder. Whatever `fielderStartAnchorFt`/`chainMoverPlan` timing governs a covering fielder's own run to the bag today has not been audited or guaranteed this session at all — it's a known gap, flagged by Alex, not yet investigated.

## Task 1 — Audit the fielding/anchor layer for coherence

Working from section A above: is the current shape (five sequential, same-condition-gated mechanisms all mutating `fielderStartAnchorFt`'s output) sound, or does it need consolidating? Specifically assess whether `flight.infieldDepthPct` and `flight.chargeAnchorFt` should be merged into one small, named "fielding adjustment" record on `flight` rather than two loosely-related properties, and whether the "no genuine crossing" condition that gates #2/#3/#5 should be computed once and threaded through, rather than (if it currently is) re-derived per call site.

## Task 2 — Extend reconciliation to every out in a multi-out play, not just the last

Design how `reconcileThrowSchedule` (or a new wrapper around it) reconciles **every** leg flagged as a real out (`schedule[i].out`) against **its own** runner — not just the schedule's final leg. This needs:
- A per-leg runner lookup, the equivalent of `forcedOutRunnerArrivalMs`/`runnerForOutTarget` but resolvable for an *intermediate* base in the chain, not just the final target.
- A decision on ordering/interaction: does reconciling leg 1 change what leg 2 needs (they share the same fielder-side knobs — `quickRelease`/`speedThrow` — potentially competing for the same headroom across two different legs' own requirements)? Work through whether the existing single-pass-at-the-end model can be extended to a per-leg pass, or whether it needs restructuring into a genuine multi-race system.
- What "the whole chain, cascading backward" (this session's `speedThrow` change) means once *multiple* legs each have their own independent required-margin target, not one shared target at the end.

## Task 3 — Design the base-coverage timing guarantee

Symmetric to Task 2 but on a *fielder's own movement to a base*, not the throw. The verdict itself is never in question — it's already fixed by the recorded result (`import_BRC`/the MLN result table decided who's out long before any of this renders, same mission constraint as everywhere else in this doc). The job here is purely making the physical story agree with that already-known out: whichever fielder is credited with recording it must be shown physically at the base, in position to receive, at or before the runner's own honest arrival — every time the result says that runner is out, no exceptions, the same "non-negotiable" standard Alex has already set for the throw side.

This covers **two mechanically distinct cases** — investigate and design for both, don't conflate them:

1. **A covering fielder receiving a throw from someone else** (Alex's own example: 1B on a grounder not hit to them). Investigate `fielderStartAnchorFt`'s non-ball-toucher branch and `chainMoverPlan`'s covering-fielder `startMs`/`ballPassesDepthMs` gate — today's mechanism, per this session's own audit, sets no `deadlineMs` at all for this entry (`chainMoverPlan` only sets `deadlineMs` when `e.pos === flight.fielder && e.base === null`, i.e. only for the ball-toucher), so a covering fielder's run to the bag has no compression and no guarantee today.
2. **The same fielder who fielded the ball carrying it in themselves, unassisted** (e.g. an unassisted 3-3 groundout, or any putout where no throw happens at all — grep `unassisted`, `unassistedLegTiming`, and `chainMoverPlan`'s own 2-leg unassisted branch). This is a pure footrace with no ball-flight timing in between at all — fielder's own charge-then-run pace vs. the runner's own honest pace — mechanically simpler than case 1 but still currently unguaranteed the same way.

**Design direction, Alex's explicit ask:** don't default to a single knob pushed to an extreme (e.g. only ever making the fielder unrealistically fast). Design this the same way Task 2's throw-side reconciliation already works — a **pooled, multi-knob correction**, mirroring `allocateAcrossKnobs`'s equal-utilization water-fill: a bounded "runner's own pace eases off a little" knob (the same shape as the existing `stretchRunner`/`hustleRunner` — a real, capped percentage, not unlimited) pooled together with a bounded "fielder's own run/charge pace picks up a little" knob (the same shape as `speedThrow` — ceilinged at a real, physically plausible top speed, not just "however fast makes the math work"). Consider explicitly whether the *existing* `stretchRunner`/`hustleRunner`/`RUNNER_HUSTLE_MAX_MS`-style constants can be reused directly for this footrace, rather than inventing parallel ones, and whether a "fielder run pace" ceiling analogous to `THROW_SPEED_BY_POS` needs its own real-world-grounded range (a barehand sprint to a bag is not the same motion as a controlled infield charge, so don't just reuse `FIELDER_CHARGE_FT_PER_S`'s own ceiling without checking whether that's the right number for a dead sprint to a base).

Investigate before designing a fix — this may turn out to be a smaller, more targeted change once Task 1's consolidation is done, or it may need its own new mechanism. Explicitly decide whether it's the same kind of fallback-only pattern already established elsewhere in this doc (only intervene when honest physics falls short, touch nothing otherwise), or something structurally different, and justify the choice.

## Task 4 — Design a direct, bidirectional fielder movement-speed knob

Alex's explicit ask, following directly from the taxonomy above: a real knob that scales a fielder's own charge/run pace up or down per play — not just achieved indirectly through the position/leeway mechanisms in section A. The immediate driver: infield hits. A ball recorded as a hit (not an out) that the honest static-anchor race would otherwise have a fielder reach in time needs a genuine way to miss it — today there's no lever for this at all outside the outfield-pursuit case (`OF_READ_DELAY_MAX_MS`/`ofDerivedShadeAnchorFt`), so an infield single currently has no correction mechanism if the honest physics comes up "too good."

Design this mirroring `slowThrow`/`speedThrow`'s own shape (a real, bounded pace range to solve within, not an unbounded multiplier), and build it bidirectional from day one, not just the hit-direction that prompted it:
- **Slower** — for a hit where honest physics would otherwise let the fielder reach it. The case that prompted this task.
- **Faster** — generalizing what Task 3's own pooled "fielder run/charge pace picks up a little" knob already needs for base-coverage, so that work and this task converge on one mechanism instead of two parallel ones.

Concretely: what's the right bounded range for a fielder's charge/run pace (distinct from `FIELDER_CHARGE_FT_PER_S`'s own single flat number today)? Is `spdPaceScale`'s existing per-player SPD scaling the right foundation to build a min/max range around (the way `THROW_SPEED_BY_POS` builds a range around each position's typical mph), or does this need its own real-world-grounded calibration pass the way `slowThrow`/`speedThrow`'s ranges did? Flag explicitly if this needs a probe/data-grounding pass before the range can be trusted, rather than picking numbers by feel. Cross-reference with Task 3 directly — the "fielder pace picks up" pooled knob that task calls for should very likely *be* this knob's fast direction, not a separate implementation.

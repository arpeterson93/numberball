## Goal

Three-part goal for this refactor, in **priority order** — (a) is inviolable; (b) and (c) are what bend when they conflict with it, never the reverse:

a) **Correctness is non-negotiable.** The rendered play must always resolve to exactly what the predetermined MLN result says happened (see mission constraint below). No design in this plan should trade this away for physical realism, ever.

b) **Physical realism is the presentation objective.** Within that constraint, the play should look like an actual baseball play: fielders start from real positions and close real distances at real speeds, runners advance basepaths at real speeds, throws travel at real velocities, reaction times are physically plausible — not a stylized approximation re-tuned per play until it "looks right."

c) **Player-attribute uniqueness, wherever the data supports it.** A fast runner should visibly run faster; a fast fielder should visibly cover more ground faster. Per the verified asymmetry above, this is already true for fielder pace but not yet for baserunning speed (and throw velocity may or may not have data to support it at all — open factual questions below) — Task 3 is about closing that gap so uniqueness applies consistently across both baserunning and fielding, not just the fielder side that already exists.

Because (a) is fixed while (b) and (c) are driven by real distances and real (or rated) speeds, pure math will routinely make the honest version of (b)/(c) disagree with (a) — a fielder or throw comes up just short of what the guaranteed result requires, or arrives with room to spare on a play that should look close. That disagreement, and how to resolve it with **one consistent, efficient, logical mechanism** instead of per-play patches, is what Task 1 decides and Task 4 designs. Every task below serves one of these three goals; keep that mapping in mind rather than treating this as an open-ended redesign.

## Project context

Numberball is a Streamlit + Supabase scouting app (repo: `C:\Users\Alex\PycharmProjects\numberball`). It ships a separate static-site game-replay viewer under `docs/` ("MLN Gameday") that animates individual plate appearances on an SVG baseball diamond.

**Mission constraint that overrides every other design decision here:** the underlying Discord league game has no physical component at all — the actual rule is "roll pitch/swing numbers, look up the result in a table." Everything visual — ball flight, fielder movement, runner speed, throw velocity, reaction times — is a layer invented on top of that already-decided result, purely to render a plausible, internally consistent physical story. **The result the table already produced must never be contradicted.** A `GO` must always resolve as a groundout, a stolen base attempt that the table says failed must always show the runner tagged out, etc. Everything below is about *how* that already-fixed outcome gets its physical story computed — never about changing what the outcome is.

**Scope boundary — read this carefully, it is the whole reason this is a separate task from the last one:** the ball's own flight (launch angle/exit velocity → distance/hang-time, 3D perspective rendering, bounce/rollout physics, and infielder depth-interception once the ball is on the ground) already went through a dedicated physics redesign on 2026-08-07 (see `physics-model-overview-for-redesign.md`, `ball-flight-3d-physics-redesign-plan.md`, `docs/js/trajectory.js`) and **is out of scope here** — do not re-litigate it, and do not touch `docs/js/trajectory.js`. This task begins where that one ends: once the ball is in flight or on the ground, how does the fielder actually get to it, how does the catch/no-catch resolve, how do runners move base-to-base, how do throws travel, and — the actual subject of this task — **what mechanism keeps all of that in sync with the result the table already decided?**

Alex's own framing of the problem (verbatim, lightly trimmed): *"I think we have so many special cases where we're trying to converge the predetermined result with the ball-flight inputs, and also the visual inputs — runner/fielder speeds, throwing speeds, reaction times. It's almost like in an ideal world we'd let the animation play out per the inputs when the result checks out, but when the numbers don't add up (fielder doesn't get to a caught ball, throw doesn't make it to a base in time) we need to make adjustments. Right now we're kind of duct-taping these fixes together, discovered by visual testing."*

That framing should shape the plan, but **don't presuppose its exact mechanism is the answer** — see Task 1. A close read of the current code (below) shows something subtly different from "physics usually agrees, we patch the exceptions": today, because the result is never actually uncertain, *every* timing in this layer is constructed backward from the known result through its own bespoke method, so there is no live moment where "honest physics" and "the result" could even be observed to disagree. The plan needs to decide whether that's the right shape (done through one shared method instead of many) or whether Alex's forward-simulate-then-patch-on-conflict model is actually what should replace it.

## Current state — what's already principled vs. what's accreted special cases

**Already per-player and physics-shaped (keep/build on these):**
- `spdPaceScale`/`fielderSpd` (app.js:2621–2637) — scales a fielder's charge pace off a real 1–5 scouted SPD rating pulled from `key_moments_build.py`'s defense fields (pitcher is a deliberate flat-average exception, since SPD is scouted for baserunning, not fielding quickness).
- `FIELDER_CHARGE_FT_PER_S=16` / `OF_CHARGE_FT_PER_S=24` (app.js:2639, 2652) and `FIELDER_ACCEL_FT_S2=25` (app.js:~3958) with `accelTimeS`/`accelDistForTimeS` — a genuine acceleration-based top-speed model, split infield-vs-outfield after a real observed play (a 76mph/18°/207ft single) exposed the infield pace as wrong for outfield closing distances.
- `chargeInIntercept`/`fielderInterceptS`/`resolveGrounderInterception` (app.js:~2682–2838) — an actual physical race (ball's ground path vs. fielder's charge) that decides *when* a grounder is fielded and by whom, not a lookup.

**Verified asymmetry — runner and throw speed are NOT per-player, despite fielder speed already being so:** `RUNNER_SPRINT_FT_PER_S=27` (app.js:1623) and `THROW_SPEED_MPH=90` (app.js:3019) are flat global constants, never run through `spdPaceScale`. `spdPaceScale`/`fielderSpd` are only ever called with a defensive position (app.js:2805, 3835, 3985) — never for a baserunner's own movement or a throwing fielder's arm strength. Worth noting: the comment at app.js:2613–2615 says the underlying per-player speed data comes from `key_moments_build.py`'s `defense`/`batter_spd`/`runners_on_base` fields — i.e. a runner speed rating may already exist in the data pipeline and simply isn't consumed on the JS side yet. Confirm this rather than assuming (see open questions).

**The backward-solved margin layer (the actual subject of this task):** app.js:2968–3093 is a block of hand-tuned millisecond constants — `RUNNER_LEAD_MS=150`, `OUT_BEAT_MS=400`, `THROW_DELAY_MS=60`, `THROW_STAGGER_MS=50`, `THROW_LEAD_MS=200` ("required margin: every throw must land at least this early"), `TAG_UP_MS=80`, `TAG_THROW_MARGIN_MS=400` — with the file's own comment at 2973–2976 stating outright that these are "deliberately left alone... there's no real-world speed to derive [them] from, only a real distance/speed pair." `TAG_THROW_MARGIN_MS`'s own history is instructive: it was bumped 200→400 (app.js:3089–3093) purely because a *correctly-decided* safe play "read as too close a race... for a play that was never actually contested" — a legibility fix with no physical referent at all.

**Concrete special cases layered on top of that:**
- `FORCED_OUT_BASE` (app.js:4431–4435) — a per-result-code lookup (`FC→"2B"`, `DPH1→"HOME"`, ...) whose own comment admits `"2B"` for `FCLead` is "an unverified default."
- `infieldSingleThrowHtml` (app.js:5017–5057) — its own comment explicitly says it's timed **backward** from the batter's known arrival at first, "instead of forward from when the ball's fielded," because the real out-throw machinery (`THROW_LEAD_MS`, `outThrowTargets`) is "built to guarantee a throw wins it" and this specific play needs a throw that's *supposed* to lose — so it's a parallel, separately-written function rather than a parameterization of the general one.
- `IF1B_DEPTH_SHIFT_FT=20` / `OF_SHIFT_DEG` (app.js:3906–3922, `fielderStartAnchorFt`) — the first baseman's *rendered starting position* is shifted 20ft off their real anchor specifically so a pitcher-covers-first play has room to visibly need the coverage; i.e. the rendering layer is compensating for the position-assignment layer not actually guaranteeing a reachable route, rather than the assignment layer itself knowing that constraint.
- `PITCHER_MIDDLE_EV_MAX_MPH` (~app.js:2841) — a discrete EV cutoff removing the pitcher from the fielding-candidate pool, rather than a reaction-time model that would naturally rule them out.
- At least three independent places re-derive/override a fielder's angle when the ball-flight-assigned fielder conflicts with `import_BRC.csv`'s required position for that situation (`applyAngleOverride`/`applyAirPositionOverride`/`resolveSinglePickup`, app.js:~2846–2941) — three parallel implementations of "make the geometry match the known assignment," not one shared mechanism.

**Scope note — relay throws are in, true cutoffs are out:** multi-leg relay throws (the ball changing hands en route to a base — e.g. a 6-4-3 double play, or any play with more than one out-bound throw) are real, already-implemented functionality: `outThrowTargets` (app.js:4558–4618) derives the ordered sequence of target bases a play's throw(s) must reach (from explicit per-play `throw_order`/`throw_order_by_position` data when present, else derived from the recorded outs), `THROW_ORDER`/`THROW_ORDER_DIGIT_TO_BASE` (app.js:4460, 4528) fix the base sequence, `coveringPosition` (app.js:4652) decides which position is drawn receiving each leg, `relayLegIsUnassisted` (app.js:3097) distinguishes an unassisted putout (same fielder covers a base himself, drawn as a jog) from a real throw, and `THROW_STAGGER_MS` (app.js:3107) sequences leg *i+1* off leg *i*'s actual landing rather than starting all legs in parallel. This is in-scope for Tasks 2–4: it uses the same distance/speed/margin timing machinery as a single throw, chained, so the unified race primitive (Task 3) and reconciliation mechanism (Task 4) need to work over an N-leg chain, not just a single fielder→base leg — and Task 2's audit should name it explicitly rather than relying on the general grep to surface it. **Explicitly out of scope, by Alex's call:** a genuine "cutoff man" mechanic — an infielder positioned in an outfield throw's path who can catch and redirect it to a different base than its original target before it arrives — does not exist today (every leg's target base is fixed in advance by `outThrowTargets`) and should not be designed here. Outfield throws always target a base directly; do not add mid-flight redirection as new scope.

**Read this fairly, not as sloppiness:** almost every constant above has a comment naming the specific observed play that exposed the problem and why the fix was scoped the way it was (the `OF_CHARGE_FT_PER_S` split is a good example: one specific 5.6-real-second gap on one specific play). That *is* the mechanism Alex flagged as the pain point — each fix is correct for the play that found it, with no shared model underneath, so the next play that hits the same underlying gap in a slightly different shape needs its own new patch. The goal of this refactor is not to declare any of this wrong, it's to find the shared principle these dozen-plus independent fixes are all reaching for and implement it once.

## Task 1 — Decide the target architecture (do this first; it's a real fork, not a formality)

Two candidate shapes, evaluate both honestly:

**(A) Forward-simulate, reconcile on conflict** (Alex's own framing): run fielder pursuit, runner sprint, and throw timing forward from real per-player rates and real distances; compare the simulated verdict/margin against the already-known result; only when they'd visually contradict (or produce an implausible margin — a "blowout" result that simulates as a photo finish, or vice versa), apply a deliberate, named adjustment.

**(B) Construct-to-agree through one shared method** (closer to what's actually implemented today, minus the current fragmentation): since the result is never actually uncertain, always build every race's timing from the known verdict outward — but through a single parameterized mechanism (one race function, one margin-selection policy) instead of the current pile of independently-invented ones.

Weigh both against: visual plausibility (does (A) risk a jarring correction snapping into place when a full-speed simulation and the guaranteed result diverge by a lot?), maintainability (does (A)'s "conflict" branch itself become a new pile of special cases, one per way physics can diverge?), and honesty to the constraint (is there ever a real "conflict" to detect, or is "the sim disagrees with the result" always just "the sim's inputs were wrong for this play," meaning (B) is what (A) collapses into anyway once you take the mission constraint seriously). Recommend one and justify it — this choice determines the shape of every task below.

## Task 3 — Design the honest per-player physical model

Close the verified asymmetry: fielder pace/acceleration is per-player (`spdPaceScale`/`fielderSpd`/`FIELDER_ACCEL_FT_S2`); runner sprint and throw velocity are flat constants. Design:
1. Whether/how `RUNNER_SPRINT_FT_PER_S` becomes per-runner, and whether a throwing fielder's arm strength becomes a real rated input to `THROW_SPEED_MPH` — contingent on the open factual question below about what data actually exists.
2. A single race/timing primitive — (distance, rate, reaction-time) in, arrival-time out — that fielder pursuit, runner base-running, and throws all go through uniformly, replacing the current situation where each has its own independently-derived formula.
3. **Stretch consideration, not required this pass — flag as an extensibility requirement on point 2's design, don't design the feature itself:** Alex may eventually want throw speed/mechanics to vary by situation, not just by distance at one flat `THROW_SPEED_MPH`. The clearest case is the PFP play already discussed in Task 5 (`IF1B_DEPTH_SHIFT_FT`, app.js:3906) — a first baseman flipping the ball underhand to a pitcher covering the bag a few feet away looks nothing like a full-effort 90mph relay throw, and today's model can't represent that difference at all, only distance-scaled duration at a constant speed. This is independent of per-player arm strength (point 1/open question 2 below): it's about a throw's *mechanics* — flip/toss vs. full throw — being appropriate to its distance and urgency. Don't design this now, but make sure the shared timing primitive from point 2 has a natural hook for a distinct lower-speed "toss" throw class later, so it doesn't require another parallel bespoke function the way `infieldSingleThrowHtml` had to be written outside the general throw machinery.

## Task 4 — Design the reconciliation mechanism

Per Task 1's chosen architecture, design the one shared method that makes a play's timing agree with the known result and read as appropriately close or decisive — replacing `RUNNER_LEAD_MS`, `OUT_BEAT_MS`, `THROW_DELAY_MS`, `THROW_STAGGER_MS`, `THROW_LEAD_MS`, `TAG_UP_MS`, `TAG_THROW_MARGIN_MS`, `IF1B_THROW_MARGIN_MS`, and the `infieldSingleThrowHtml`/`tagStart`-style backward-solves. This needs an explicit answer for: **what determines how close a given play should look** — a stolen base thrown out by a step vs. a routine 6-3 groundout are both "outs" but should read completely differently. Is there real data to ground this (something in the league's own numbers that already implies margin), or does it stay a hand-authored "how close is this result" table? If the latter, design it as one explicit, documented table/policy — not scattered magic constants each with their own provenance comment.

## Task 5 — Push fixes upstream where the fix belongs upstream

For every constant Task 2 classifies as (c) — a downstream compensation for an upstream gap — design the upstream fix instead. `IF1B_DEPTH_SHIFT_FT`/`fielderStartAnchorFt`'s cosmetic starting-position fudge is the clearest example: if 1B's real anchor plus real speed genuinely can't make it back to the bag in time on a PFP play, that's a fact the fielder-assignment/coverage logic (already reworked once in the prior ball-flight redesign — `resolveGrounderInterception` and friends) should know and act on, not something the rendering layer should fake by moving where the token starts.

**Second concrete example, explicitly worth a genuinely open design pass rather than just tuning the constant:** the same function's outfield branch (`OF_SHIFT_DEG`/`OF_SHIFT_ARCHETYPES`/`OF_CANONICAL_ANGLE`, app.js:3912–3922) is how today's system keeps an outfielder from visually looking like they should have caught a ball that the result says fell for a hit — every outfielder's rendered starting anchor gets nudged angularly away from the ball's landing direction by a flat `OF_SHIFT_DEG` on any play in `OF_SHIFT_ARCHETYPES` (or a whitelisted `single` result), purely so there's "room" on screen. This is the same category of fix as the PFP case — a downstream cosmetic compensating for the fact that nothing upstream actually guarantees a fielder starting from their true anchor, moving at their true pursuit speed, would fail to reach the ball. Alex is specifically interested in whether there's a better mechanism here than shifting the start position, and wants the plan to genuinely explore alternatives rather than default to "keep the shift, derive its size more honestly." Candidate directions worth weighing (not a prescription — pick, combine, or reject based on which best serves Goal (b)'s realism bar without breaking Goal (a)):
- An honest outfield pursuit race, the same shape as the grounder-interception race already built for infielders (`chargeInIntercept`/`resolveGrounderInterception`, app.js:~2682–2838): start the fielder at their *real* anchor with no positional fudge at all, run their real closing speed/acceleration/reaction time against the ball's real hang time and landing point, and let a genuine "does the honest route get there in time" computation decide — consistent with whichever architecture Task 1 lands on.
- If some form of "give it room" adjustment is still needed under Task 1's chosen architecture, derive its size per play from that specific fielder's real speed/reaction time and the ball's actual hang-time/landing geometry, instead of a flat `OF_SHIFT_DEG` angular nudge applied uniformly regardless of how set up (or not) the play actually is for a nearby fielder.
- A "reading the ball off the bat" reaction-time cost, distinct from the routine `CHARGE_REACTION_S`-style beats already in the model, if a physically fast fielder still needs a plausible reason not to arrive — i.e., sell the miss through a believable hesitation rather than through where the token starts.
Whichever direction the plan recommends, it should replace the shift for every archetype currently in `OF_SHIFT_ARCHETYPES`/`OF_SHIFT_WH_SINGLE_RESULTS`, not just the case that happens to be easiest.
